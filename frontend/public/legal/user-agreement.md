This summary is not the agreement — it is here so you know what you are agreeing to. If the summary and the full text differ, the full text applies.

- **RydrSafe cannot make you safe.** Use your own judgement, and in an emergency call **10111**.

## 1. Acceptance, and who this covers

## 2. What RydrSafe is — and what it is not

2.1 RydrSafe is an independent community safety platform. Passengers upload screenshots from e-hailing apps; we read driver and vehicle details off those screenshots using optical character recognition (OCR); we check those details against reports other passengers have submitted; and we show the user a verification result.

2.2 **RydrSafe is not affiliated with, endorsed by, sponsored by, or connected to Uber, Bolt, inDrive, DiDi, or any other ride-hailing company.** Where we mention those names, we do so only to describe where a screenshot came from.

2.3 RydrSafe **is not**:

- a criminal record check, a background screening service, or a vetting service;
- a law-enforcement body, or a body charged by law with applying criminal law;
- a credit bureau or a registered credit reporting agency;
- an emergency service;
- a substitute for reporting a crime to the South African Police Service.

2.4 **Emergencies.** If you are in danger, call **10111** (SAPS), **112** from a mobile phone, or **10177** (ambulance). The GBV Command Centre is **0800 428 428**. RydrSafe does not monitor the service in real time and cannot dispatch help.

3.3 You may not create an account on behalf of another person, or hold more than one account, without our written permission.

## 5. Verification — how it works and what it cannot tell you

5.1 You upload one or more screenshots. We extract the driver name, vehicle registration number, vehicle make and model, and phone number where visible. The image is then deleted (see clause 10 and Part B, clause 25).

5.2 We match those details against our records using both exact and fuzzy matching, because OCR misreads characters. Fuzzy matching means **we sometimes match the wrong person.** A different driver with a similar name, or a registration number misread by one character, can produce a false match.

5.3 A verification result tells you what has been reported and corroborated on this platform. It does not tell you whether a driver is safe. **The absence of reports is not evidence of safety** — most drivers on the road have never been reported to us at all.

5.4 You must form your own judgement about whether to take a ride. Do not use a "clean" RydrSafe result as a reason to ignore your own instincts.

## 6. Reports — and when they become visible to others

This clause is the core safeguard of the platform. It exists because publishing an unproven allegation of criminal conduct about a named person is both a defamation risk and a restricted activity under POPIA.

### 6.1 Report categories

Reports fall into two groups:

**Category A — alleged criminal conduct.** Assault · Theft · Fraud · Intoxicated Driving · Harassment · Reckless Driving · and any report categorised as *Other* until a moderator reclassifies it.

**Category B — safety and conduct concerns.** Unsafe Vehicle · and any *Other* report a moderator has reclassified as not alleging an offence.

### 6.2 Publication stages

Every report moves through these stages. **No report is visible to other users when you submit it.**

| Stage | What it means | Who can see it | Effect on the driver's public status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pending** | Newly submitted. Awaiting moderation. | You and our moderators only. | None. |
| **Approved** | A moderator is satisfied the report is genuine, internally consistent, and not obviously malicious. | You and our moderators only. | None yet for Category A. Category B may show as a generalised concern. |
| **Corroborated** | The corroboration threshold in clause 6.3 is met. | Other users, in the limited form described in clause 6.4. | Contributes to the public risk indicator. |
| **Rejected / Withdrawn** | Not accepted, or withdrawn. | You and our moderators only. | None. Excluded from all scoring. |

### 6.3 The corroboration threshold

A **Category A** report becomes visible to other users only when at least one of the following is true:

(a) **two or more independent approved reports** in the same or a related category name the same driver, submitted by accounts that are not linked to each other. We test that linkage using only two signals — a one-way hash of the submitting IP address and a one-way hash of a device identifier — both of which are described in Part B, clause 20.1. We do not use payment or referral data for this, because we do not collect any; or

(b) the reporter has supplied a **SAPS case number (CAS/AR)** or other official reference for the incident, and a moderator has confirmed the reference is well-formed and consistent with the report; or

(c) the conduct is **already a matter of public record** in a form the data subject deliberately made public, or which was lawfully published by a court, tribunal or regulator.

A **Category B** report may be shown in generalised form on a single approved report.

**A single uncorroborated allegation of criminal conduct is never shown to other users.** It is retained, privately, so that a second independent report can corroborate it.

### 6.4 What other users actually see

Where a report is published, other users see only:

- the **category** and **severity band**;
- the **number** of corroborated reports;
- the **date range** in which the incidents were reported.

Other users **never** see the free-text description you wrote, your name, your account, or any detail that would identify you or the specific trip. We do not publish descriptions because a description is where defamatory detail lives.

**Reporting anonymously.** The report form offers an anonymity option, and it is worth being precise about what it does, because "anonymous" is a word platforms routinely oversell.

Ticking it marks your report as one where you asked not to be named, and that request is shown to the moderator reviewing it. What it does **not** do is sever the report from your account in our database. It cannot, for three reasons:

- **Clause 6.3(a) depends on it.** Two reports from the same account must never corroborate each other. If reports were genuinely unlinked, one person with one account could manufacture corroboration at will, and the single safeguard this platform's legal position rests on would stop working.
- **Withdrawal depends on it.** Clause 6.2 lets you withdraw your own report. We can only offer that if we can tell which reports are yours.
- **Clause 37 depends on it.** Where someone reports maliciously or repeatedly in bad faith, we have to be able to act on the account behind it.

So: anonymity here means your name is withheld from other users — which clause 6.4 already guarantees for every report — and your request not to be named is recorded and respected. It does not mean we do not know who you are. If you want us never to hold your real identity in the first place, clause 4.6 is the route, and the trade-offs there are real.

### 6.5 Right of reply

Before a driver's public status changes to *Flagged* or *High Risk*, we will, where we have a usable contact detail for that driver and where doing so does not put a reporter at risk, notify the driver and give them a reasonable opportunity to respond. Their response is considered by a moderator before the status changes.

### 6.6 Our discretion

We may reject, edit for compliance, redact, or remove any report. We may decline to publish a report even where the threshold in clause 6.3 is met, if we judge publication unfair, unsafe, or unlawful. We are not obliged to publish anything.

## 7. Risk scores, and the right to a human decision

7.1 A risk score is generated from the number of corroborated reports, their severity, how recent they are, and their verification status. Bands are: Safe (0–29), Under Review (30–59), Flagged (60–79), High Risk (80–100).

7.2 **A risk score is not a criminal record, a conviction, a finding of fact, or a legal determination.** It is a summary of what this community has reported and what our moderators have accepted.

7.3 **Human review.** Under section 71 of POPIA, a person may not be subjected to a decision with legal consequences, or which affects them to a substantial degree, based solely on automated processing. Accordingly:

(a) the algorithm may compute a score, but **a moderator must approve any change to a driver's publicly visible status**;
(b) a driver may require that any status affecting them be reviewed by a person, and may make representations to that person (Part C, clause 35);
(c) we keep a record of who made each status decision and on what basis.

## 8. Your obligations when you report

By submitting a report you warrant that:

8.1 You **personally experienced or directly witnessed** the incident. Do not report something a friend told you, something you read online, or something you inferred.

8.2 Everything you state as fact is **true to the best of your knowledge**, and you have a genuine and reasonable belief in it.

8.3 You distinguish **what happened** from **what you concluded**. "The driver's speech was slurred and the car smelled of alcohol" is a fact you observed. "The driver was drunk" is a conclusion. Report the first.

8.4 You have **not altered, staged, or fabricated** any screenshot or evidence.

8.5 You are **not using RydrSafe to retaliate** over a fare dispute, a bad rating, a cancelled trip, a personal argument, or a rejected advance.

8.6 You are not reporting on the basis of the driver's race, nationality, ethnicity, home language, religion, gender, sexual orientation, disability, or immigration status. **Reports that are, in substance, complaints about who a driver is rather than what a driver did will be rejected and may result in a ban.** South Africa has a specific and serious problem with xenophobic targeting of foreign-national e-hailing drivers, and this platform will not be a vehicle for it.

8.7 You accept that we may ask you for further detail, and that we may reject your report if you do not respond.

That weighing has to be done and recorded — a Legitimate Interests Assessment — and it has to be done *before* the processing it authorises, not asserted afterwards.

> **Status: the Legitimate Interests Assessment is not yet complete.** Until it is, this clause states the basis we intend to rely on, not one we have established. We would rather say that than claim an assessment we have not done, which would be evidence against us rather than for us.

The assessment we will record weighs the following. Passengers have a real and serious interest in avoiding drivers who have harmed others. Drivers have a real and serious interest in not being labelled dangerous on thin evidence, and the consequence for a driver who is wrongly labelled falls on their livelihood. The safeguards in Part A, clause 6 — no publication of uncorroborated criminal allegations, no publication of free text, human review, right of reply, and a working appeals process — are what we say brings the balance out in favour of processing. **If those safeguards are not operating, the basis fails.** They are not decorative. The assessment will be reviewed at least annually and made available to the Information Regulator on request.

## 24. Special personal information — allegations of criminal conduct

This clause states our position squarely, because it is the most legally sensitive thing this platform does.

24.1 **The prohibition.** Section 26(b) of POPIA prohibits the processing of personal information concerning a person's criminal behaviour, to the extent it relates to the alleged commission of an offence, unless one of the authorisations in Chapter 3, Part B applies. Reports in **Category A** (Part A, clause 6.1) are exactly that: allegations of offences against identifiable people. A moderator's belief that a report "looks genuine" is not a lawful authorisation.

24.2 **What we rely on.** We rely on:

(a) **Section 27(1)(b)** — processing necessary for the establishment, exercise or defence of a right or obligation in law. Passengers have a constitutional right to freedom and security of the person, including the right to be free from all forms of violence (section 12 of the Constitution). A reporter recording an incident, and a passenger checking before entering a stranger's vehicle at night, are exercising and protecting that right. We limit ourselves to what is necessary for that purpose: corroborated categories and counts, not narratives, not identities.

(b) **Section 27(1)(e)**, in the narrow case where the information was deliberately made public by the data subject.

(c) **Section 33**, where information was obtained in accordance with the law.

24.3 **Prior authorisation from the Regulator.** Section **57(1)(b)** of POPIA requires a responsible party to obtain **prior authorisation from the Information Regulator** before processing information on criminal behaviour, or on unlawful or objectionable conduct, **on behalf of third parties**. We take the view that this applies to RydrSafe: the allegations come from passengers, concern drivers, and are processed for the benefit of other passengers.

(Section 57(1)(d) concerns the transfer of special personal information to a third country and is a different obligation. An earlier draft of this clause cited it in error.)

24.4 **The consequence for you as a user.** The section 58(2) standstill covers *processing*, not publication alone — submitting, storing, moderating, matching and scoring are all processing. So while it is in force, **we do not accept Category A reports at all.** The form will tell you the category is unavailable and why. We do not quietly take the report and hold it: taking it would itself be the thing we are not permitted to do.

Reports in Category B — vehicle safety and conduct that alleges no offence — are unaffected and work normally throughout.

We consider this the correct trade-off, and we are aware it makes the product less useful in the meantime. A platform that processes unproven criminal allegations because it is convenient is a platform that ruins innocent people's livelihoods. If you are reporting a crime, report it to SAPS on **10111**; we are not a substitute for that and never were.

## 25. Screenshots and OCR

25.1 Uploaded images are held **in memory only**, for the duration of the request that processes them, solely to extract text. They are never written to disk and never written to our database.

25.2 The memory holding an image is cleared and released before the verification request returns — so "deleted immediately" here means within the seconds that request takes, not within a retention window. We record the moment it happened against your verification record, so the promise is evidenced rather than asserted. We do not keep an image archive, and there is no image for us to produce later, to anyone.

25.3 What survives an upload is: the text fields we extracted (driver name, registration number, phone number, vehicle make and model where visible), and a SHA-256 hash of the image. We use the hash to detect the same screenshot being submitted repeatedly to manufacture corroboration. A hash cannot be turned back into a picture.

25.4 **Where the extracted text goes.** Be aware that the extracted text is not confined to the verification response:

(a) it is stored on your verification history record, so you can see what you checked and when;
(b) where the extraction matches a driver we already hold, the match may update that driver's record;
(c) the image is sent to our OCR operator (clause 27.3) to be read, and is subject to that operator's own retention, which we describe there.

Each of these is covered by the retention periods in clause 29, after which the OCR-derived fields are stripped from the record.

25.5 Alert notifications sent to users who follow a driver carry that driver's name and current public status band only. They do not carry text freshly extracted from your screenshot, the registration number you uploaded, or any phone number.

25.6 The single exception to 25.1: where an upload is the subject of an active fraud investigation, a dispute, or a lawful request from a court or law-enforcement authority, we may retain the extracted data and hash for that specific upload for as long as strictly necessary, and we log why. This exception does not create a retained image — there is none.

## 26. Automated processing and the risk score

26.1 The risk score is produced by an algorithm using the number of corroborated reports, their severity, their recency and their verification status.

26.2 Under **section 71 of POPIA**, no driver's publicly visible status is set by the algorithm alone. A moderator reviews and approves every change to a driver's public status, and records the reasons.

26.3 A driver may ask for the score affecting them to be explained, may make representations about it, and may require human reconsideration (Part C, clause 35).
