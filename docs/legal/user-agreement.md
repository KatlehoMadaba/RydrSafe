## 2. What RydrSafe is — and what it is not

2.1 RydrSafe is an independent community safety platform. Passengers upload screenshots from e-hailing apps; we read driver and vehicle details off those screenshots using optical character recognition (OCR); we check those details against reports other passengers have submitted; and we show the user a verification result.

2.2 **RydrSafe is not affiliated with, endorsed by, sponsored by, or connected to Uber, Bolt, inDrive, DiDi, or any other ride-hailing company.** Where we mention those names, we do so only to describe where a screenshot came from.

2.3 RydrSafe **is not**:

2.4 **Emergencies.** If you are in danger, call **10111** (SAPS), **112** from a mobile phone, or **10177** (ambulance). The GBV Command Centre is **0800 428 428**. RydrSafe does not monitor the service in real time and cannot dispatch help.

## 5. Verification — how it works and what it cannot tell you

5.1 You upload one or more screenshots. We extract the driver name, vehicle registration number, vehicle make and model, and phone number where visible. The image is then deleted (see clause 10 and Part B, clause 25).

5.2 We match those details against our records using both exact and fuzzy matching, because OCR misreads characters. Fuzzy matching means **we sometimes match the wrong person.** A different driver with a similar name, or a registration number misread by one character, can produce a false match.

5.3 A verification result tells you what has been reported and corroborated on this platform. It does not tell you whether a driver is safe. **The absence of reports is not evidence of safety** — most drivers on the road have never been reported to us at all.

5.4 You must form your own judgement about whether to take a ride. Do not use a "clean" RydrSafe result as a reason to ignore your own instincts.

## 6. Reports — and when they become visible to others

This clause is the core safeguard of the platform. It exists because publishing an unproven allegation of criminal conduct about a named person is both a defamation risk and a restricted activity under POPIA.

### 6.1 Report categories

Reports fall into two groups:

**Category A — alleged criminal conduct.** Assault · Theft · Fraud · Intoxicated Driving · Harassment · Reckless Driving · and any report categorised as *Other* until a moderator reclassifies it.

**Category B — safety and conduct concerns.** Unsafe Vehicle · and any *Other* report a moderator has reclassified as not alleging an offence.

### 6.2 Publication stages

Every report moves through these stages. **No report is visible to other users when you submit it.**

| Stage | What it means | Who can see it | Effect on the driver's public status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pending** | Newly submitted. Awaiting moderation. | You and our moderators only. | None. |
| **Approved** | A moderator is satisfied the report is genuine, internally consistent, and not obviously malicious. | You and our moderators only. | None yet for Category A. Category B may show as a generalised concern. |
| **Corroborated** | The corroboration threshold in clause 6.3 is met. | Other users, in the limited form described in clause 6.4. | Contributes to the public risk indicator. |
| **Rejected / Withdrawn** | Not accepted, or withdrawn. | You and our moderators only. | None. Excluded from all scoring. |

### 6.3 The corroboration threshold

A **Category A** report becomes visible to other users only when at least one of the following is true:

(a) **two or more independent approved reports** in the same or a related category name the same driver, submitted by accounts that are not linked to each other by device, network, payment identity or referral; or

(b) the reporter has supplied a **SAPS case number (CAS/AR)** or other official reference for the incident, and a moderator has confirmed the reference is well-formed and consistent with the report; or

(c) the conduct is **already a matter of public record** in a form the data subject deliberately made public, or which was lawfully published by a court, tribunal or regulator.

A **Category B** report may be shown in generalised form on a single approved report.

**A single uncorroborated allegation of criminal conduct is never shown to other users.** It is retained, privately, so that a second independent report can corroborate it.

### 6.4 What other users actually see

Where a report is published, other users see only:

- the **category** and **severity band**;
- the **number** of corroborated reports;
- the **date range** in which the incidents were reported.

Other users **never** see the free-text description you wrote, your name, your account, or any detail that would identify you or the specific trip. We do not publish descriptions because a description is where defamatory detail lives.

### 6.5 Right of reply

Before a driver's public status changes to *Flagged* or *High Risk*, we will, where we have a usable contact detail for that driver and where doing so does not put a reporter at risk, notify the driver and give them a reasonable opportunity to respond. Their response is considered by a moderator before the status changes.

### 6.6 Our discretion

We may reject, edit for compliance, redact, or remove any report. We may decline to publish a report even where the threshold in clause 6.3 is met, if we judge publication unfair, unsafe, or unlawful. We are not obliged to publish anything.

## 7. Risk scores, and the right to a human decision

7.1 A risk score is generated from the number of corroborated reports, their severity, how recent they are, and their verification status. Bands are: Safe (0–29), Under Review (30–59), Flagged (60–79), High Risk (80–100).

7.2 **A risk score is not a criminal record, a conviction, a finding of fact, or a legal determination.** It is a summary of what this community has reported and what our moderators have accepted.

7.3 **Human review.** Under section 71 of POPIA, a person may not be subjected to a decision with legal consequences, or which affects them to a substantial degree, based solely on automated processing. Accordingly:

(a) the algorithm may compute a score, but **a moderator must approve any change to a driver's publicly visible status**;
(b) a driver may require that any status affecting them be reviewed by a person, and may make representations to that person (Part C, clause 35);
(c) we keep a record of who made each status decision and on what basis.

## 8. Your obligations when you report

By submitting a report you warrant that:

8.1 You **personally experienced or directly witnessed** the incident. Do not report something a friend told you, something you read online, or something you inferred.

8.2 Everything you state as fact is **true to the best of your knowledge**, and you have a genuine and reasonable belief in it.

8.3 You distinguish **what happened** from **what you concluded**. "The driver's speech was slurred and the car smelled of alcohol" is a fact you observed. "The driver was drunk" is a conclusion. Report the first.

8.4 You have **not altered, staged, or fabricated** any screenshot or evidence.

8.5 You are **not using RydrSafe to retaliate** over a fare dispute, a bad rating, a cancelled trip, a personal argument, or a rejected advance.

8.6 You are not reporting on the basis of the driver's race, nationality, ethnicity, home language, religion, gender, sexual orientation, disability, or immigration status. **Reports that are, in substance, complaints about who a driver is rather than what a driver did will be rejected and may result in a ban.** South Africa has a specific and serious problem with xenophobic targeting of foreign-national e-hailing drivers, and this platform will not be a vehicle for it.

8.7 You accept that we may ask you for further detail, and that we may reject your report if you do not respond.

## 33. What we hold, and where it came from

33.1 If passengers have uploaded screenshots of trips with you, we may hold your name, your phone number where it appeared, your vehicle registration, make and model, and any reports about you.

33.2 That information came from passengers, not from you or from any e-hailing operator. We have no data-sharing arrangement with Uber, Bolt, inDrive, DiDi or anyone else.

33.3 **Find out what we hold:** go to `[LEGAL URL]/drivers`, or email `[DRIVER EMAIL]`. We will verify your identity before disclosing anything — this protects you.

## 34. Your rights

You may:

- **Access** everything we hold about you, in an intelligible form, and be told who has had access to it;
- **Correct** anything inaccurate, incomplete, misleading or out of date;
- **Delete** anything that is irrelevant, excessive, out of date, or unlawfully obtained;
- **Object** to our processing on reasonable grounds, using Form 1 of the POPIA Regulations;
- **Appeal** any moderation or scoring decision (clause 35);
- **Require a human decision** rather than an automated one (POPIA s71);
- **Complain to the Information Regulator**, or take us to court.

We do not charge for this. We respond within **30 days**.

## 35. Appeals

35.1 You can appeal: an individual report, your risk score or status band, a mistaken identity or duplicate profile, or a refusal of an earlier request.

35.2 **How:** email `[APPEALS EMAIL]` with your name, your vehicle registration, what you are disputing, and anything supporting your version — a trip record, a roadworthy certificate, an operator record, a police report, a statement. You do not need a lawyer.

35.3 **What we do:**

| Step | Timeframe |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge your appeal | 2 business days |
| **Suspend public display** of the disputed item while we review it | Immediately |
| Review by a moderator who was not involved in the original decision | 10 business days |
| Written outcome, with reasons | With the decision |
| Escalation to the Information Officer if you are unhappy | 10 further business days |

35.4 **The disputed item is hidden from other users while we review it.** We would rather temporarily hide something true than leave something false in public.

35.5 If we uphold your appeal we remove or correct the information, recompute your score, and tell you what changed. If we dismiss it, we tell you why, in writing, and tell you how to escalate.

## 36. Mistaken identity

36.1 We match on names and registration numbers read by OCR, which makes errors, and different people share names and change vehicles.

36.2 If you believe a report belongs to someone else, tell us. We will immediately hide the disputed link and investigate. Where we cannot establish with reasonable confidence that a report concerns you, **we remove the link to you.** The burden is on the platform to justify the match, not on you to disprove it.

## 37. Reporters who abuse the platform

37.1 We monitor for coordinated, repeated and retaliatory reporting, and we ban accounts that do it.

37.2 If a court orders us to disclose the identity of a person who defamed you, we will comply with that order. We will not disclose a reporter's identity without a lawful basis, because doing so would expose people to retaliation.
